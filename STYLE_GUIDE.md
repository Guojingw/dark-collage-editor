# Dark Collage Editor — Style Guide

This file is the visual reference for the ChatGPT Project.

## 1. Visual identity

Dark editorial collage with punk / goth / emo / zine influence.

The style should feel:
- designed rather than randomly cluttered
- tactile rather than glossy
- graphic but still photographic
- rebellious without becoming unreadable
- cohesive across a series

## 2. Palette

**Dominant**
- black
- charcoal

**Accent**
- deep red
- oxblood
- dried-blood red

**Contrast**
- dirty white
- off-white
- photocopy gray

Skin may retain natural tone when it helps preserve identity.

Avoid turning the whole image into flat bright red.

## 3. Portrait hierarchy

The portrait is the hero.

A good composition usually contains:
- one dominant source portrait
- zero to two small portrait fragments
- a larger amount of non-portrait collage material

The human should read first.
The collage should read second.

## 4. Texture vocabulary

Use only some of these at once.

Good options:
- torn paper
- photocopy grain
- halftone
- rough paper fibers
- film grain
- scratches
- red paint
- ink splatter
- tape
- marker scribbles
- pencil / chalk marks
- distressed print
- chain / cross / hardware motifs

Do not max out every texture simultaneously.

## 5. Good chaos

Good chaos has:
- asymmetry
- a clear focal point
- uneven red accents
- cropped type
- intentional overlap
- directional movement
- negative space
- different texture scales

Bad chaos has:
- five or more repeated faces
- every corner filled
- random readable phrases
- face obscured by accident
- identical stickers everywhere
- no dominant subject
- the same layout repeated for every photo

## 6. Portrait fragments

Use fragments only when they add composition.

Good:
- one eye crop as a small torn insert
- one hand crop
- one alternate profile
- one small black-and-white duplicate from the user's own uploaded photo

Bad:
- rows of repeated heads
- AI-invented eyes
- unrelated faces
- portrait fragments larger than the main subject without a reason

When uncertain, use fewer.

## 7. Typography

Typography is graphic structure, not filler.

Preferred:
- distressed serif
- fragmented blackletter
- photocopied sans serif
- typewriter label
- stencil
- rough marker writing
- cut-paper lettering

Use exact user-supplied wording.

If no text is requested, rely on texture and abstract marks rather than fake readable copy.

## 8. Composition ideas

Vary between images:
- large centered portrait + left torn edge
- portrait pushed to one side + large negative space
- diagonal torn-paper band
- one small inset portrait
- oversized background word
- no portrait inset, only paint and texture
- horizontal spread with type at the edge
- tight crop with minimal graphics

A batch should share a visual language but not a template.

## 9. Preset feel

### Clean
Photo first, collage second.

### Editorial
Balanced photo and design. This is the default and the main signature.

### Chaotic
Dense zine / poster energy, but still controlled and source-faithful.

## 10. Final visual test

Ask:
- Is the person still clearly the subject?
- Does the image feel designed, not merely busy?
- Is the red intentional?
- Are portrait fragments sparse?
- Does this look like part of the same series without copying the exact previous layout?

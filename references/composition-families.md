# Composition Families — Dark Collage Editor v3

Choose one primary family from the TARGET image geometry. Do not choose from habit and do not copy the STYLE_REFERENCE layout literally.

## Selection logic

Use these cues:

| Target cue | Prefer |
|---|---|
| clear full / half body, disposable background | A — Hero Cutout |
| meaningful environment or strong natural split | B — Split Photography |
| large negative space, poster-like framing | C — Oversized Type Field |
| close-up with one strong face / hand detail | D — Torn Portrait Collision |
| subject pushed to one side, strong gaze / arm direction | E — Graphic Negative Space |
| extreme close-up, little background available | F — Tight Crop Poster |

When two families both fit, choose the one that preserves identity with less subject reconstruction.

## A — Hero Cutout

Best for:
- clear full / half-body portrait
- readable silhouette
- source background is visually disposable

Structure:
- preserve the TARGET person as the photographic anchor
- visually separate or strongly isolate the subject from the source background
- let the person occupy roughly 55–75% of the frame
- place one large black / dirty-white / oxblood structure behind the subject
- let one tear or red gesture interact with shoulder, hair, arm, or clothing edge
- keep the face photographically faithful

Strong move:
- one large torn field behind the body plus one directional red counterweight

Avoid:
- sticker-cutout outline around the entire body
- floating subject with no grounding mass
- symmetrical framing
- changing clothes or body shape while “cutting out”

## B — Split Photography

Best for:
- part of the source environment is useful
- strong wall / shadow / doorway / furniture / horizon / architectural split
- identity preservation benefits from keeping more of the source intact

Structure:
- retain one meaningful photographic field
- transform another field into black / off-white / red zine structure
- use a hand-torn seam as the material handoff
- allow halftone / xerox / paint to cross the seam selectively
- keep the main subject continuous across the design

Strong move:
- one irregular vertical, diagonal, or horizontal tear that follows source geometry

Avoid:
- clean 50/50 digital split
- preserving the entire original photo and merely adding side panels
- clipping directly through eyes, nose, mouth, or fingers

## C — Oversized Type Field

Best for:
- strong negative space beside / behind the subject
- fashion or poster-like portrait
- user supplies exact text
- or the user explicitly permits abstract typographic texture

Structure:
- use one oversized word / cropped letter field as a Macro mass
- let the subject overlap or interrupt the type
- add one torn-paper counterfield or restrained red structure
- maintain large-scale readability

If user says No text:
- do not use this family unless it can be executed with non-letter graphic bars instead

If user says No readable text:
- only abstract, damaged, incomplete letterforms are allowed

Avoid:
- multiple slogans
- fake magazine paragraphs
- covering defining facial features
- making typography the first read instead of the person

## D — Torn Portrait Collision

Best for:
- close-up portrait
- a strong eye, half-face, hand, profile, or hair detail exists in the same TARGET
- one secondary portrait fragment genuinely improves the composition

Structure:
- main TARGET portrait remains dominant
- use at most one medium secondary fragment in Editorial
- the fragment must come from the same TARGET by default
- convert the fragment to xerox / monochrome / halftone if useful
- connect main portrait and fragment with one strong vertical or diagonal torn seam
- use oxblood red as a bridge, not a full-image wash

Strong move:
- color main portrait + one monochrome source crop + tactile torn boundary

Avoid:
- alternate face invented by the model
- borrowing a face from a STYLE_REFERENCE
- mirrored-face gimmicks
- more fragments when one is sufficient

## E — Graphic Negative Space

Best for:
- horizontal image
- subject sits to one side
- strong gaze / arm / body direction
- one large low-information area exists

Structure:
- preserve the subject side strongly
- convert the opposite side into a large black or dirty-white field
- place one oxblood gesture, halftone block, or torn structure in that field
- use the subject direction to guide the graphic field
- keep significant quiet space

Strong move:
- one calm graphic field plus one sharp red intervention

Avoid:
- filling the quiet field with small icons and scratches
- edge-only collage
- forcing the subject back to center

## F — Tight Crop Poster

Best for:
- extreme close-up
- intimate portrait
- main face already dominates
- little room exists for background rebuilding

Structure:
- preserve face, hairline, and defining skin detail
- allow one or two large torn intrusions from edges into the non-critical facial perimeter or background
- use one black / off-white / red Macro block
- keep Micro texture low around eyes, nose, and mouth
- optional same-target fragment only away from the main face

Strong move:
- large-scale crop + one torn material intrusion + one red/black counterform

Avoid:
- turning the main face into a xerox illustration
- heavy halftone over eyes or lips
- synthetic skin smoothing
- facial reconstruction for “editorial” effect

## Family modifiers

These are not separate families. Use at most one when useful:

- **Red Rim** — a narrow oxblood edge that follows a hair / shoulder / clothing silhouette
- **Xerox Window** — one rectangular or torn photocopy patch behind the subject
- **Tape Bridge** — one tape strip connecting two paper fields
- **Hardware Accent** — one small chain / cross / safety-pin-like motif
- **Dry-Brush Vector** — one red brush gesture aligned with pose or gaze

Do not stack all modifiers.

## Batch distribution

For 4–9 TARGET images:
- avoid repeating the same family on adjacent pages
- keep at least three distinct macro geometries when the source set permits
- use fragments selectively; not every page needs one
- alternate denser and quieter pages
- preserve each image's original orientation

A series should feel like one zine issue with different page designs, not a template deck.

## Final family test

Before generation ask internally:

1. Does this family follow the TARGET geometry?
2. Can it preserve the face, hands, clothes, and pose?
3. Does it create a real interior composition change?
4. Is the strongest graphic move different from the previous batch image?
5. Could the same design have been chosen without seeing the TARGET?

If answer 5 is yes, the family choice is too generic. Re-select.

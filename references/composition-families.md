# Composition Families — Dark Collage Editor v2

Use one primary family per image. Pick it from the source geometry.

## A — Hero Cutout

Best for:
- clear full / half-body portrait
- simple silhouette
- source background not worth preserving

Structure:
- visually separate the person from the original background
- person occupies about 55–75% of the frame
- place one large black / dirty-white / deep-red paper field behind them
- let one tear or red graphic gesture interact with shoulder, hair, arm, or silhouette
- keep the face untouched

Avoid:
- centered sticker-cutout look
- symmetrical border
- floating person with no grounding structure

## B — Split Photography

Best for:
- source background has some mood worth retaining
- portrait occupies one side or one clear region
- strong vertical / horizontal / diagonal source division exists

Structure:
- preserve roughly 35–60% of the original photographic field
- transform the remaining field into black / white / red zine structure
- use one tear as the material handoff
- allow xerox / halftone to cross the boundary selectively

Avoid:
- perfect 50/50 digital split
- clean vector masks
- keeping the whole original photo intact

## C — Oversized Type Field

Best for:
- strong negative space beside / behind the subject
- poster-like portrait
- user supplies text, or abstract unreadable type is allowed

Structure:
- use one oversized cropped word / letter field as a macro mass
- keep type behind or beside the portrait
- allow letters to be partially hidden by the subject
- combine with one torn-paper field and restrained red

If No text:
- use non-readable letter fragments only

Avoid:
- multiple slogans
- dense magazine copy
- covering defining facial features

## D — Torn Portrait Collision

Best for:
- close-up portrait
- strong face / eye / hand detail
- image can support one secondary source-derived fragment

Structure:
- main portrait remains dominant
- use one narrow or medium source-photo B&W fragment from the same supplied photo
- connect the two with a strong vertical / diagonal torn edge
- use deep red as a bridging mark, not as a full wash

Avoid:
- invented alternate face
- three or more face fragments
- mirrored-face gimmicks unless source-derived and user-requested

## E — Graphic Negative Space

Best for:
- horizontal images
- subject already sits to one side
- portrait has a strong gaze or arm direction
- large calm background area exists

Structure:
- preserve the subject area strongly
- convert the opposite side into one large black / dirty-white field
- place one red structural gesture or halftone block in that field
- use negative space deliberately

Avoid:
- filling the quiet field with small decorations
- edge-only collage

## F — Tight Crop Poster

Best for:
- extreme close-up
- intimate portrait
- source face is already large
- little room exists for full background rebuilding

Structure:
- preserve face and defining hair exactly
- use one or two torn intrusions from edges that enter the composition
- add a single black / white / red macro block
- keep micro texture low around eyes, nose, and mouth
- optional one source-derived fragment away from the main face

Avoid:
- over-processing skin
- photocopying the main face unless user asks
- turning the face into illustration

## Batch distribution

For 4–9 images, aim for visual variety such as:

- 1–2 Hero Cutout
- 1–2 Split Photography
- 1 Oversized Type Field
- 1 Torn Portrait Collision
- 1 Graphic Negative Space
- 1 Tight Crop Poster when a close-up supports it

Do not force every family to appear. The source image decides.

Two adjacent outputs should not use the same macro geometry unless the series clearly benefits from a deliberate pair.
